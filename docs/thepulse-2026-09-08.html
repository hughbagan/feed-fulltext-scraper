On the agenda: Derelict properties, budget engagement, Valley Line LRT

Sept. 8, 2026
By Stephanie Swensrude and Mack Male

This week, council will consider tax subclasses for derelict properties, review public engagement on the 2027-2030 budget, and vote on funding for the Valley Line LRT transition and equipment for the expanding network.

There is a city council meeting on Sept. 8, with a continuation on Sept. 9 if required.

Here are some key items on the agenda this week:

  • Council will vote on the first reading of a bylaw that would establish a citywide tax subclass for derelict non-residential properties that have been unoccupied for at least a year. Administration estimates implementation would require up to $274,000 in annual operating funding starting in 2027 and up to $1 million in capital funding, with those requests to be considered during the 2027-2030 budget deliberations. Council will also consider all three readings of a bylaw expanding the existing derelict residential subclass from mature neighbourhoods to the entire city. Funding for that expansion is already approved. If the bylaw passes, eligible properties across Edmonton will be included starting with the January 2027 assessment notices.
  • Participants in public engagement on the 2027-2030 budget wanted a focus on core services but disagreed on what "core" means, says a report due at council. Of the 1,467 residents who used an online budget tool, 42% kept property tax revenue the same, 40% increased it, and 18% decreased it. More than half of budget-tool participants chose spending cuts for police, environment and climate, and a combined category covering arts, culture, tourism, inclusion, and economic development. Administration will use the feedback to help shape the proposed four-year operating and capital budgets.
  • Council will be asked to approve funding for a transition management office to transfer the operations and maintenance of the Valley Line Southeast LRT from TransEd to the Edmonton Transit Service in 2027. Administration is seeking about $7.7 million for the temporary office and $20.5 million for vehicles, tools, equipment, and operating systems for Valley Line Southeast and other LRT expansions, all from the LRT reserve. The request excludes the payment to terminate the TransEd contract.

Headlines: Sept. 8, 2026

Sept. 8, 2026
By Mariam Ibrahim
  • Indigenous actor and filmmaker Georgina Lightning has died at 63 after being struck by a horse during an Indian Relay event at Enoch Cree Nation west of Edmonton. Lightning was filming a documentary about the relay sport, a project she was passionate about. Known for roles in Trickster and Blackstone, she was also celebrated as a trailblazer, mentor and advocate for Indigenous communities. Her family plans to have the unfinished documentary completed to preserve her legacy.
  • The University of Alberta is ending funding for six affiliated non-profit child-care centres, The Tyee reported. Parents who study at the school say the move could affect their ability to complete their studies. The university announced the $298,600 funding cut in 2025, citing financial pressures and rising costs, while the centres warn the loss could lead to reduced services and staff layoffs. Critics have also questioned why the university continues to maintain a corporate membership with for-profit child-care provider Kids & Co., which is available to faculty and staff, but not students.
  • The Nest, the on-campus restaurant at NAIT, has been closed indefinitely after carbon monoxide was detected on Sept. 3, leading to two hospitalizations. An ongoing investigation involving NAIT, technical experts, and an industrial hygienist aims to identify the carbon monoxide source. NAIT spokesperson Veronica Jubinville confirmed that there is no broader campus risk and that monitoring measures are in place.
  • Edmonton's the outdoor pool season is ending, with Borden, Fred Broadstock, and Mill Creek pools closing Sept. 8, the City of Edmonton said in a release. Wîhkwêntôwin and Queen Elizabeth pools expected to close by Sept. 13, weather permitting. The City said 93,023 visits were recorded by Sept. 2.
  • A fire broke out at a vacant Parkview home in the early morning hours of Sept. 7, hours after nearly 200 residents gathered across the street to protest infill projects in the Edmonton neighbourhood. Edmonton Fire Rescue Service crews responded around 3 a.m. to the property, which is one of seven homes sold for redevelopment into more than 50 multi-family units. Residents say the projects threaten the area's character and could worsen traffic, waste collection and property-value concerns. The fire remains under investigation.
  • Edmonton Coun. Michael Janz is proposing a fee on developers for each tree removed during construction of new neighbourhoods on the city's outskirts. Janz argues developers currently do not account for the environmental costs of clearing forests and wetlands, leaving taxpayers to fund tree planting and other climate-related measures. The proposal would apply to new developments, while a separate bylaw under consideration would address tree removal on established properties and infill sites.
  • The Edmonton Marathon has apologized after organizers confirmed that 13 runners who appeared to qualify for the Boston Marathon will not be able to compete there next year. The Aug. 16 Edmonton course was found to be 674 metres longer than the standard marathon distance, making runners' times ineligible for adjusted qualifying standards. Boston organizers agreed to recognize 92 runners who met the qualifying mark based on their recorded times, but rejected adjusted times that could have allowed the 13 affected runners to qualify. Edmonton Marathon and Athletics Canada say they are reviewing course-certification procedures and will introduce additional safeguards to prevent similar errors.
  • A new Edmonton facility will provide 40 affordable housing units for veterans experiencing homelessness. Veterans' House Edmonton will offer subsidized rents, community spaces and access to mental-health, employment and life-skills supports. Organizers say veterans account for about 7% of Alberta's homeless population. The facility is expected to open shortly after Remembrance Day.
  • Alberta Chief Justice Ritu Khullar, who also led the courts of appeal for Alberta, Nunavut and the Northwest Territories, has died after an illness, the Court of Appeal of Alberta said in a statement issued Sept. 4. Khullar, who died on Sept. 3 surrounded by family, spent more than two decades in private practice before being appointed to the Court of King's Bench in 2017. She became chief justice of Alberta and the three territories' courts of appeal in 2022.
  • Elections Alberta has received more than 37,000 applications but remains short of the 60,000 workers it says are needed for the Oct. 19 referendum. Recruitment is particularly difficult in rural and northern areas, while the 10-question vote is expected to create longer lineups and a lengthy counting process. About 45 million ballots will be printed and hand-counted within 48 hours. Meanwhile, nearly 290,000 Albertans have applied for mail-in ballots.
